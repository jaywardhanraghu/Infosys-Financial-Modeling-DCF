# Infosys Ltd. — Financial Statement Modeling & DCF Valuation

## Overview

This project develops an integrated **three-statement financial model and equity valuation of Infosys Limited (NSE: INFY)** using ten years of historical financial data and a five-year forward forecast.

The analysis follows the workflow used in **Equity Research, Investment Banking, Corporate Finance, FP&A, and Financial Analytics**:

**Historical Financial Analysis → Driver-Based Forecasting → Three-Statement Model → FCFF → WACC → DCF → Comparable Company Analysis → Sensitivity & Scenario Analysis**

The objective is to estimate Infosys' intrinsic value and evaluate the valuation implied by the current market price.

# 6. Free Cash Flow to Firm

The valuation uses a **Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF)** framework.

The core calculation is:

**FCFF = EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate) + D&A − CapEx − Change in NWC**

Where:

- EBIT = Earnings Before Interest and Tax
- Tax Rate = Effective operating tax assumption
- D&A = Depreciation and Amortization
- CapEx = Capital Expenditure
- NWC = Net Working Capital

FCFF represents the cash flow available to all capital providers before financing decisions.

This makes FCFF appropriate for an enterprise-value-based DCF.

---

# 7. WACC Estimation

The model estimates the Weighted Average Cost of Capital using CAPM and the company's capital structure.

### Cost of Equity

**Cost of Equity = Risk-Free Rate + Beta × Equity Risk Premium**

### After-Tax Cost of Debt

**After-Tax Cost of Debt = Pre-Tax Cost of Debt × (1 − Tax Rate)**

### WACC

**WACC = E/(D+E) × Cost of Equity + D/(D+E) × After-Tax Cost of Debt**

The base-case DCF uses:

### **12.2% WACC**

The discount rate is one of the most important valuation assumptions and is therefore stress-tested extensively in the sensitivity analysis.

# 8. Discounted Cash Flow Valuation

The DCF discounts projected FCFF over the FY2027–FY2031 forecast period and calculates terminal value using the perpetual-growth method.

↓

**Enterprise Value**

↓

**− Debt**

↓

**+ Cash & Investments**

↓

**Equity Value**

↓

**÷ Shares Outstanding**

↓

# 13. Investment Conclusion

Infosys demonstrates a strong long-term financial profile, with approximately **11.2% revenue CAGR**, **23.7% FY2026 operating margin**, and **12.8% operating cash-flow CAGR** over the historical period analyzed.

The company has also maintained relatively modest borrowings relative to its operating scale, while generating substantial operating cash flow.

However, the valuation framework indicates that **strong underlying business fundamentals do not automatically imply attractive equity valuation**.

Under the base-case FCFF DCF:

- Intrinsic value is estimated at **₹881/share**
- Reference market price is **₹1,191/share**
- Implied downside is approximately **26%**

The sensitivity analysis demonstrates substantial valuation dispersion, with modeled intrinsic values ranging from approximately **₹771 to ₹1,775/share** depending on WACC and terminal-growth assumptions.

The key investment conclusion is therefore:

> **Infosys exhibits strong long-term fundamentals, profitability, and cash-generation characteristics, but the attractiveness of the equity depends materially on the relationship between future growth, margin performance, and the valuation multiple investors are willing to pay. Under the base-case DCF assumptions used in this model, the market price implies a valuation above estimated intrinsic value.**

The analysis should therefore be interpreted as a **valuation framework rather than a definitive price target**, with the final conclusion highly sensitive to assumptions surrounding growth, margins, WACC, terminal growth, and future free cash flow.
